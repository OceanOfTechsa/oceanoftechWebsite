### What drives the price up?

The biggest cost multipliers — in order of impact:

1. **Custom design vs. adapted template** — custom UI design can add $5,000–$20,000 before a line of code is written
2. **Number of unique page layouts** — each distinct layout is design and dev work
3. **Integrations** — CRM, payment, analytics, email, auth add days of work each
4. **CMS requirements** — custom content management setups take significant backend time
5. **Animations and interactions** — polished motion UI can double the frontend timeline
6. **Revisions and scope creep** — the single biggest hidden cost in most projects

The price gap between a freelancer and an agency is real, but so is the difference in what you get. Here's an honest breakdown:

### Choose a freelancer if:
- Your scope is clearly defined and unlikely to change
- You have time to manage the project yourself (or enjoy doing so)
- You need something built quickly and budget is the constraint
- You already have designs and just need implementation

### Choose an agency if:
- You need design, development, and strategy in one engagement
- You want a team with a proven process and accountability
- The website is a core business asset — not just a brochure
- You want ongoing support, maintenance, and growth capability
- You don't have time to manage a project yourself

> **The hidden cost of cheap:** A poorly built website isn't just a design problem. Slow load times cost you SEO rankings. Poor mobile experience costs you conversions. Code that's hard to maintain costs you money every time you want to make a change. The cheapest option upfront is rarely the cheapest option over 2 years.

## 5. Red Flags When Hiring

<img
    src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1600880292203-757bb62b4baf?w=1200&q=80"
    alt="Business meeting discussing website project requirements"
    className="w-full rounded-sm  my-6 h-64 object-cover"
/>

After seeing a lot of projects go wrong, these are the warning signs that reliably predict a bad outcome:

- **No contract or vague SOW** — if the scope of work isn't written down, you'll pay for everything twice
- **No portfolio of real, live websites** — mockups and Dribbble shots don't prove shipping ability
- **Lowest quote by a large margin** — extreme underbidding means something will be cut, usually quality or scope
- **Can't explain the technology choices** — "I'll use WordPress" with no rationale isn't a technical decision
- **No conversation about ongoing maintenance** — a website needs updates, security patches, and content changes
- **Promises specific Google rankings** — SEO timelines are never guaranteed; anyone who says otherwise is lying
- **No process for revisions and feedback** — without a structured feedback loop, projects spiral

## 6. How to Get the Most from Your Budget

Whether you have $5,000 or $50,000, these principles maximise what you get:

**Invest in content before design.** Most website projects stall because the client doesn't have copy, images, or a clear message ready. Copy drives design — know what you're saying before you design how it looks.

**Prioritise speed over perfection.** A good website live in 6 weeks beats a perfect website live in 6 months. You'll learn more from real users in two weeks than from any internal review.

**Separate launch from done.** Plan your website in phases. Launch with what you need to convert visitors, then iterate. Trying to launch everything at once is how budgets explode.

**Own your infrastructure.** Your domain, hosting account, and CMS login should always be in your name. Never let an agency or freelancer own these on your behalf.

**Ask about handover.** At the end of the project, you should be able to make basic content changes yourself, or hand the site to any other developer. Proprietary systems that lock you in are a liability.

## The Short Version

If you've read this far and just want the decision framework:

- **Under $3k budget:** Use Framer or Webflow. It's the honest answer.
- **$3k–$10k:** Hire a vetted freelancer with a strong portfolio. Define scope in writing.
- **$10k–$40k:** Work with a small agency or senior freelance team. Expect strategy, design, and dev.
- **$40k+:** Agency engagement with a full process — discovery, design system, development, launch, and retainer.
- **Ongoing product:** Consider in-house after product-market fit, not before.

The website you need today is probably not the website you'll need in two years. Build what solves your problem now, build it properly, and leave room to grow.
